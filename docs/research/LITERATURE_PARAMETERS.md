### Classification column

- **observed-in-source**: the number is stated in the source. If the source is quoting another study, the original reference is named in the "population/context" column as "secondary, orig. ...". These secondary numbers are only as reliable as the original, which was not checked here.
- **derived**: computed here from numbers in the source. The arithmetic is shown.

### Cautions that apply throughout

1. Both Adekunle reviews (A&K 2026, Adekunle 2026) are narrative or scoping reviews. Nearly every number in them is secondary. Adekunle 2026 says it did not pool effect sizes: "Quantitative data were not pooled or synthesized through meta-analytic techniques" (p. 7).
2. **The $23.60 figure is a BRD treatment cost, not a metaphylaxis price.** Rojas uses it as the cost of one metaphylactic treatment. Its own source is the NAHMS Beef Feedlot 2011 info sheet, which reports it as the direct cost of treating a respiratory case (J&P 2017 p. 2; Rojas ref. 34).
3. Rojas's metaphylaxis efficacy of 0.5 is an assumption. It cites Avra et al. 2017, which is a study of treatment-failure risk factors, not a metaphylaxis trial.
4. Rojas's abstract says 188,188 cattle, but the body text and Table 3 arithmetic say 188,118 (p. 1 vs pp. 2 and 10).

## Gaps: parameters the simulation needs that these sources do not provide (must be assumptions)

1. **Adjusted effect sizes (OR / RR / HR) for every arrival risk factor.** None of the six sources gives a numeric, adjusted effect for: arrival weight as a continuous variable, sex (steer / heifer / bull / mixed), arrival quarter, auction vs ranch-direct source, number of sources, transport distance or hours, vaccination history, weather (temperature swing, cold, heat, dust), pen density, bunk space, cohort size, or BVDV-PI exposure. Rojas used several of these as predictors but published no coefficients. The only magnitudes available are secondary review figures: preconditioning (−40 to 55% treatments; −50 to 60% morbidity; −30 to 35% mortality), Hereford 10x, and threshold definitions (≥6 h transport; commingling <12 days). The risk-multiplier table must be assumed or taken from primary literature outside this set (e.g., Sanderson 2008; Babcock 2013; Hay 2014/2016/2017; Snowder 2006; Cernicchiaro 2012).
2. **How cohort morbidity is distributed.** Rojas gives only the means (6.30% overall; 27.07% high-risk; 3.77% low-risk) and the 10.85% high-risk share. No variance, histogram, or beta / zero-inflated parameters. Overdispersion between and within lots is not given.
3. **Daily hazard or epidemic curve within 0 to 45 DOF.** Only coarse shares are given (65 to 80% of cases in the first 45 DOF; Q2 heifer FDO 20 to 80 days; DTD right-skewed). No peak day, distribution shape, or parameters for time to first treatment.
4. **Metaphylaxis efficacy.** The only number is Rojas's assumed 50% reduction in morbidity. Nothing is given for mortality reduction, how efficacy differs by drug or risk class, how it changes with timing (on arrival vs delayed), or how long protection lasts. A&K says only "substantially reduces."
5. **Real metaphylaxis drug cost.** $23.60 is the NAHMS 2011 treatment cost per case, which Rojas reuses. Per-drug prices, dose-by-weight, and processing or labor cost are not given.
6. **Treatment cost per case by treatment number** (1st / 2nd / 3rd) and drug class. Only $23.60 per case, plus the older $2 to $12.39 range.
7. **Detection sensitivity and specificity in the feedlot, at the animal level, for visual pen riding vs lung lesions.** Only secondary pooled DART figures (Se 62 to 67%, Sp 63 to 70%). Nothing is given for detection delay in days, how Se/Sp change with pen-rider workload or lot size, or the share of subclinical cases.
8. **Accuracy of AI or sensor surveillance in feedlot beef cattle.** The sensor figures quoted (accelerometers about 3 days early) come from preweaned dairy calves. No validated feedlot Se/Sp, lead time, false-alarm rate, or cost per head for PLF systems.
9. **Case fatality and treatment response by risk class or placement weight.** NAHMS 2011 response rates (via J&P) are national, whole-period, pooled numbers. Weber gives mortality by placement weight but not by treatment status within the 45-day window.
10. **Chronic and cull rates inside the 45-DOF window.** Weber's CULL values apply to the whole feeding period. J&P chronic rates are whole-period too.
11. **Transmission parameters** (β, γ, σ, R0, latent period) for within-lot spread. A&K gives the SEIR-Q structure with no values.
12. **Weber's missing pieces:** Tobit mortality coefficients, finished-weight distributions (γk, σ), clear units for ADG / AFC / VCPH, and the treatment mix (TRT1X, TRT2X, TRT3X) expected for a given 45-day morbidity.
13. **NAHMS 2019 does not report** the % of cattle individually treated for BRD, the drug classes used for individual BRD treatment, BRD morbidity or mortality, or group-treatment rates broken down by placement weight. It gives only % of feedlots treating any sick animal individually (80.0%; small 78.5%, large 97.9%) and group-treatment drug use.
14. **Quantified AMR trade-off.** No resistance prevalence (%), no rate of resistance gain per metaphylaxis use, and no cost of resistance per head. AMR is described qualitatively only.
15. **Labor, hospital-pen and pull costs**, and an economic value for welfare: not given.
16. **Price inputs that match the current market.** Weber's prices are fixed values (fed $1.2628/lb; feeder $1.4558 to $1.7397/lb; corn $0.0923/lb) and their date basis is not stated. Price volatility is not given.
17. **Interactions between risk factors** (e.g., light weight × auction × fall). Adekunle 2026 says these are absent from the literature.
